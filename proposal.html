Portfolio proposal · October 2026

A quieter home
for your work.

Your photographs set the tone. The website gives them space, makes your range easy to explore, and stays simple to update.

This working concept follows your Figma direction: a white canvas, bold name, generous spacing and uncropped images. It adds ways to explore disciplines, look closer and understand a complete project.

01

The experience

  • A curated homepage with discreet discipline filters.
  • Large image viewing, with keyboard navigation.
  • Project pages for stories, image sequences and credits.
  • Short silent loops, plus videos with playback controls.
  • About and contact pages, designed for phone and desktop.

Choose the homepage selection and order yourself. Standalone photographs and project covers share the same gallery and discipline filters. A discreet “Project ↗” label identifies a cover that opens a complete series, with its story, image sequence and credits. Standalone photographs open in the large image viewer.

The concept demonstrates this with Chef Therapy, in Art direction. You would choose which images belong to each project, its cover and the order of the series.

Explore the example project: Chef Therapy ↗
02

Easy to keep current

The finished website would include a content editor for uploading and replacing images, arranging work, choosing covers, assigning disciplines and adding tags.

Tags help organise the library. Only the categories useful to visitors need to appear on the website. Editing content keeps the established visual layout.

This concept demonstrates the visitor experience. The publishing editor will be built as part of the commissioned project.

03

Detail, without the wait

Small, optimised images for browsing; larger versions when someone looks closer. Media loads as it becomes useful, rather than downloading the whole portfolio at once.

Large collections will appear gradually in small batches, without numbered pages. A limited automatic load can keep browsing smooth, followed by a clear “Load more” option so the footer stays reachable. Returning from a project or image view will preserve your place and filter.

GIF-like animations can retain their feel in efficient video formats. Full videos start when requested, with controls. The final exports will be reviewed against the originals for colour and detail.

The prototype shows a sample collection; incremental gallery loading will be implemented in the finished site. Performance targets: good Core Web Vitals, no image-driven layout jumps, and measured checks on mobile. Results depend on the approved media and hosting.

Your choice: who moves the content?

You migrate the content — 44–68 hours of development. We configure the editor and media handling, add representative examples, and show you how to publish. You select, upload and organise your photographs and videos, add categories, descriptions and credits, and choose covers and order. Editor training and launch checks remain included.

We migrate the content — 50–80 hours in total. We also populate the agreed initial collection from the files and copy you provide, ready for your review.

Doing the migration yourself could remove an estimated 6–12 hours of paid migration work. You would invest your own time in preparing and entering content, and launch would depend on that collection being ready. You can request help with a smaller part of the collection; we would agree that scope and cost separately.

Planning estimates, rather than final quotations. The optional migration allowance assumes up to 60 organised photographs, the current 8 loops and 4 videos, and up to 3 project stories, with usable source files and approved copy. A larger catalogue will be estimated after review. Both options include two consolidated rounds of visual feedback. Specialist file recovery or unusual manual conversion would be assessed separately.

The fixed development price will be quoted in euros after we confirm the content volume and scope. Hosting, domain and email will be listed separately. There is no required monthly maintenance retainer; additional support can be requested and quoted when needed.

05

Keeping running
costs low

A custom static website with a hosted content editor can start at €0 in base hosting and CMS fees, within the providers’ free-plan limits. Domain, email, optional video services and future usage are additional.

As an alternative to commissioning this custom website, you could keep and refine your existing Adobe Portfolio. If your current Adobe subscription already includes it, the platform may add no extra fee. Its themes and editing controls would set the design limits. Adobe Portfolio is not a planned integration or a required subscription for the custom website.

Two videos in this prototype use the existing Adobe players. Final video hosting will be chosen separately, based on quality, cost and the subscriptions you want to retain.

We will confirm the lowest practical recurring cost after checking your existing subscriptions, media volume and how you want to publish. Paid services will be approved before setup.

06

Guarantee
& support

Delivery defects will be corrected at no charge, with no time limit. This covers reproducible faults attributable to the delivered implementation against the agreed scope, even if discovered later. No maintenance subscription is needed to use this guarantee.

New features, content work, and adaptations required by later changes to external services are separate work. We will assess the cause together before treating a request as paid support, and agree any charge in advance.

Support is available on demand. There is no mandatory monthly fee.

The current contact address, raffy@dishtag.com, depends on the domain and email service. Those need a separate retention or migration decision before anything is removed.
